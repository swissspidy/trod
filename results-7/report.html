What we learned

  1. Experience with other questions does not replace the question's own hint. Neither distilled notes nor the raw past questions moved accuracy significantly, although the agents read them (memory was searched in 328 of 330 runs). The hint itself is worth 7 points.
  2. BIRD's missing knowledge is question-specific. Counting a task as solved when a majority of its seeds pass, the hint fixes only 10 of the 110 tasks; memory also fixes 4 of those. The hints that matter often encode one-off choices ("average monthly consumption = AVG(Consumption) / 12", "John as their first name = full_name LIKE 'John%'"); the best-matching train hint shares on average 45% of the words of the hint that fixed a task. About 30% of tasks fail even with their hint, consistent with BIRD's known annotation noise, so the headroom is small.
  3. The notes still make sessions cheaper. With notes, Haiku used 18% fewer tokens (82k → 67k) and 23% fewer turns (4.4 → 3.4): it skipped schema exploration it would otherwise do. On moderate questions they helped (36 → 45 of 78 runs) and on simple ones they cost a little (157 → 152).
  4. Where it sits against round 6b. AppWorld's missing knowledge was a dozen recurring conventions, so a 3 KB note reached most tasks (+7.5 points, p = 0.02). BIRD's is a long tail of per-question definitions, so the same approach mostly saves tokens. A forged library or note pays in proportion to how much of tomorrow's work today's lessons cover.
