Do self-made tools pay off?

trod is an MCP server for Claude Code that turns files into live tools, so an agent can build its own tools mid-session. These experiments measure, in headless claude -p sessions, whether that makes agents cheaper or better than plain Bash.

Short answer: not as a mid-task trick, but yes as an asset. Round 4: Haiku with a tool library that Opus forged once got every answer right in all 9 runs. Sonnet from scratch did so in 6 of 9. Haiku did it at 5–14% of Sonnet's cost. Round 5: on SWE-bench (Django) the same approach does nothing, because the models already know Django; the gains come from knowledge that cannot be guessed. Round 6: on AppWorld a tool library ties, but the same forged lessons as always-loaded notes lift Haiku from 87.5% to 95%. Round 7: on BIRD text-to-SQL the same idea only saves tokens, because the missing knowledge is specific to each question. Round 8: on ScienceWorld, where whole procedures recur, forged notes take Haiku from 52% to 89%, past Sonnet. Round 9: on MiniWoB++ web tasks, notes forged from practice episodes alone take Haiku from 68% to 90% on the hardest task types, again past Sonnet; notes Haiku forged for itself reach 88%. Round 10: the notes need no routing: given only an index, Haiku reads the right page itself every time (89.3%). Round 11: trod learn distils notes from ordinary past sessions, no setup: 68% → 81–83% from 120 transcripts (with or without labels, Opus or Haiku as learner), 88% after one more round. Round 12: scheduled, incremental learning with an evidence ledger; a third round reaches 91.3%, more runs solved than the practice notes.

Round 16: handing off from Opus to Haiku mid-task saves nothing

27 SWE-bench Verified Django issues. Opus investigates and may hand the fix to Haiku: 26/27 at $0.160 per task, against 26/27 at $0.151 for Opus alone and 13/27 for Haiku alone. Given the choice, Opus finished the fix itself in 26 of 27.

Round 15: validated tuning on AppWorld finds nothing to improve

Three rewrites of the notes by Opus, each rejected on held-out dev tasks; one would have cost 3.5 points. The unchanged notes re-measured at 117/120 (97.5%) on the test sample.

Round 14: MiniWoB++ at 98.6% of what can be scored

An exact right-angle tool and more practice seeds: 143/150 on the test seeds, every solved run with a reward above 0.999, and 140/150 on five new seeds. One task type cannot be scored at all.

Round 13: validated harness tuning on MiniWoB++

Practise, let Opus propose new notes, keep them only if they win on held-out seeds: 141/150 solved (94%), up from 137, with 136 at full credit instead of 122.

Round 12: scheduled learning with an evidence ledger

A third round of ordinary sessions, then incremental trod learn that checks every rule against the new sessions (46 rules: 35 kept, 4 fixed, 5 new, 2 dropped). Haiku solves 91.3% (round 2: 88.0%, practice notes: 90.0%, no notes: 68.0%) for $6.12 of learning in total; 39 fixed, 4 broken against no notes.

Round 11: trod learn, notes from sessions that already happened

No brief, no success check, no practice environment: trod learn condensed 120 ordinary Haiku sessions (58 passed) and compared failures with successes. Haiku solves 68.0% → 82.7% (27 fixed, 5 broken, p = 1×10⁻⁴); without labels 80.7%; with Haiku as its own learner ($0.44) 81.3%. After one more round of sessions and learning: 88.0%, equal to live practice notes forged by Haiku.

Round 10: a wiki of notes routes itself

The 29 round-9 notes as wiki pages behind a 3.2 KB index in CLAUDE.md. Haiku read its own task type's page in 145 of 145 runs, never a wrong one, and solved 89.3% (pre-routed: 90.0%; no notes: 68.0%). Loading all 29 pages at once solves as many (90.7%) at 1.9× the cost per run.

Round 9: MiniWoB++: notes forged from practice take Haiku from 68% to 90%

30 hard web task types × 5 test seeds. Opus practised each type on train seeds with only a success signal and wrote rules ($8.01 for 30). Haiku solves 68.0% → 90.0% (37 fixed, 4 broken, p = 1×10⁻⁷) with 33% fewer tokens, past Sonnet without notes (67.3%) at 9% of its cost per run. Round 9b: Haiku as its own teacher ($0.79 for 30 types) reaches 88%. One note games the grader (draw-circle); without it, 70% → 90%.

Round 8: ScienceWorld: forged notes take Haiku from 52% to 89%, past Sonnet

30 task types × 2 test variations × 3 seeds of a text-based science simulator. Per-task-type notes forged by Opus from train variations ($6.55): Haiku solves 52.2% → 88.9% (67 fixed, 1 broken, p = 5×10⁻¹⁹) with 39% fewer tokens, beating Sonnet without notes (66.7%) at a tenth of the cost per run.

Round 7: BIRD text-to-SQL: knowledge that does not transfer

110 questions on 11 real databases, 3 seeds. Notes forged from earlier questions, or the earlier questions themselves, leave Haiku at 61–63% (not significant); the question's own expert hint gives 68.5%. BIRD's missing knowledge is per question, so the notes mostly save tokens (−18%).

Round 6: AppWorld: the tools tie, the distilled notes win

40 scenarios × 3 variants of everyday app chores, graded by database-state tests. A forged tool library ties (Haiku 87.5% either way). The same lessons as an always-loaded CLAUDE.md: 95.0% (11 tasks fixed, 2 broken, p = 0.022), at no extra tokens. The forge's useful output was knowledge, not code. Round 6d: notes that trod forge wrote itself reach only 86.7–90%; the 95% hinged on a few rules one forge found.

Round 5: SWE-bench Verified (Django): no effect, and why

67 instances, 670 graded runs, one Opus forge. The library changed neither model's resolve rate (Haiku 79.6% → 79.1%, Sonnet 82.8% → 83.6%) and cost 15–22% more tokens. The models already know how to work in Django. Forged libraries help in proportion to how much of an environment's working knowledge cannot be guessed.

Round 4: forge once with Opus, run with Haiku

trod forge plus library cards and code mode. Haiku with the forged library: 9/9 perfect runs (Sonnet from scratch: 6/9), at 5–14% of Sonnet's cost; 0.21–0.69× Haiku's own tokens. Each forge pays for itself after about 12–22 sessions.

Round 4: the Haiku runs

Haiku 5.5 from scratch vs Haiku with the Opus-forged library: turns 45 → 25, 54 → 20 and 39 → 25, up to 6× faster.

Round 1: does an agent build tools on its own, and do they pay?

120 sessions, trod 0.1. Agents almost never built tools unprompted (1 in 24). When required to, sessions cost 1.84× plain Bash: a script on disk is already a tool.

Round 2: trod 0.2 within a session

Single-file tools, trod_run, a validation hook. Unprompted tool building in 16 of 18 sessions; overhead down to 1.25×, parity on one task. The library arm was the only arm with no failures.

Round 2b: a cheaper model with a stronger model's tools

Haiku 5.5 with tools Sonnet built: 0.64× tokens on average and 0.23× on code-qa, with perfect scores and far less variance.

Round 3: a tool library across fresh sessions

Ephemeral sessions against a quirky internal API. A library built once in a dedicated session: 0.74× tokens per session, no failures, break-even after about 12 sessions.
