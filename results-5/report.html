A real coding benchmark: no effect, and why

All 67 SWE-bench Verified Django 4.0/4.1 instances that grade correctly in this environment, in fresh checkouts with no git history, graded with the official FAIL_TO_PASS and PASS_TO_PASS tests. trod forge built a Django library once with Opus 5.5 ($2.29), from the repository only, at a commit before every instance: a compact test runner, a configured snippet runner, AST source lookup, grep and test discovery. Sessions then ran with that library (cards and trod_eval) or from scratch: Haiku 5.5 with 3 seeds, Sonnet 5.5 with 2 seeds, 670 graded runs in total.

The library made no difference. Haiku resolved 79.6% from scratch and 79.1% with the library; Sonnet resolved 82.8% and 83.6%. Paired, there were 8 vs 7 and 3 vs 4 discordant runs (p = 1). The library cost 15–22% more tokens. Sonnet used the tools in only 17 of 134 sessions, Haiku in 70 of 201.

Why it worked in round 4 and not here: the library pays off where the environment holds knowledge the model cannot guess, such as an internal API's auth header, its number formats, or an unseen import graph. Django is the opposite. The models already know how to work in it: Haiku ran Django's own test suite in 66 of 67 sessions without any help, and Sonnet fixed most issues in about 5 turns without running tests. What is left is reasoning about a localized bug, and tools do not do that. Public-repository benchmarks like SWE-bench are close to the worst case for this approach. A separate observation, independent of trod: Haiku reached about 96% of Sonnet's resolve rate at about 1/6 of the cost.
