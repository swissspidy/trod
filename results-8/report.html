What we learned

  1. When knowledge recurs, forged notes are a big lever. Each task type's notes cover what every variation needs: that focus on takes the object as the observations name it (focus on substance in metal pot, not focus on water), that sinks and stoves can be broken and where the fallback is, how long to wait, how to read a thermometer, which pairings of paints make which colors. A newcomer has to discover all of that by trial and error within 100 actions; Haiku with the notes simply does it.
  2. The notes beat a bigger model. Haiku + $0.22 of notes per task type outperforms Sonnet without them by 22 points, at a tenth of the cost per run.
  3. This completes the picture from rounds 5–7. Forged knowledge pays in proportion to how much of tomorrow's work today's lessons cover: nothing where the model already knows the domain (SWE-bench, round 5), little where every question needs its own fact (BIRD, round 7), +7.5 points where a dozen conventions recur (AppWorld, round 6b), and +37 points where whole procedures recur (ScienceWorld).
